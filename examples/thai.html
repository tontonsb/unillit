Reading tips for place names
No word boundaries
Thai is written without spaces between words. Spaces mark phrase or clause boundaries. In short place names this rarely causes ambiguity, but longer names may need to be parsed syllable by syllable.
Inherent vowel
A consonant with no visible vowel sign has an inherent short o (or a in some environments). The inherent vowel is deleted in closed syllables. e.g. กรุงเทพ = Krung Thep (not Ko-ro-ung...).
ph / th / kh = aspirated
These are NOT f/voiced-th/ch. ph = aspirated p (as in pot), th = aspirated t (as in top), kh = aspirated k (as in kin). The unaspirated counterparts are plain p/t/k.
Final consonant mergers
All stops reduce to three final sounds: /k/, /t/, /p/. Many consonants share the same final — e.g. ก ข ค ฆ all end as /k/. Sonorants (ง น ม ย ว ร ล) keep their value or become a vowel glide.
Common prefixes in place names
บาง Bang- (waterway/settlement)  ·  นคร Nakhon (great city)  ·  บุรี Buri/Puri (city)  ·  เขา Khao (mountain)  ·  แม่ Mae (river/mother)  ·  อ่าว Ao (bay)
RTGS vs. common spellings
Official RTGS romanisation often differs from established English spellings: Krung Thep (not Bangkok), Chiang Mai (official; RTGS would be Chiang Mai), Phuket (from ภูเก็ต Phu Ket). Older maps may use pre-1999 RTGS conventions.
Romanisation follows RTGS 1999 as used on Thai road signs and official maps. Tones are not marked in RTGS. Thai Unicode block: U+0E00–U+0E7F
